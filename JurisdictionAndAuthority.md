# Jurisdiction and Authority: An Etymological Reconstruction

---

## Etymology of *Jurisdiction*

The word **jurisdiction** denotes the legal authority or power to exercise authority, interpret law, and administer justice within a defined geographic area or sphere of responsibility.

---

## Latin (Direct Origin)

The word derives from the Latin noun *iurisdictio* — "administration of justice" or "legal authority." It is a compound of two terms:

- **iūs** (genitive *iūris*): "law," "right," or "justice"
- **dictiō** (from *dicere*): "the act of saying" or "speaking"

Literally: *the speaking of the law.* The compound entered Old French as *jurisdiction* and reached Middle English in the 14th century.

---

## Proto-Indo-European (Deepest Roots)

Both Latin components trace to Proto-Indo-European roots:

- **The Law root** (*iūs*): from PIE *\*yewes-*, meaning "law" or "binding oath"
- **The Speaking root** (*dictiō*): from PIE *\*deyk-*, meaning "to show," "to point out," or "to pronounce solemnly"

---

## Greek Cognates (Parallel Evolution)

English did not borrow *jurisdiction* from Greek, but Greek shares the same PIE roots along a parallel track:

- PIE *\*deyk-* evolved into Greek *deiknynai* (δείκνυμι), "to show" or "to point out"
- And into *dikē* (δίκη), meaning "custom," "usage," or "justice"
