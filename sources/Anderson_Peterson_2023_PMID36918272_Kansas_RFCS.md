# Rear-Facing Child Safety Seat Effectiveness: Evidence from Motor Vehicle Crash Data

**Authors:** Anderson DM, Peterson RW (Montana State University / NBER)  
**Published:** Inj Prev. 2023 Aug;29(4):320-326  
**DOI:** 10.1136/ip-2022-044815  
**PMID:** 36918272  
**Source URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36918272/  
**Data Source:** Kansas Department of Transportation MVC data, 2011-2020  
**Status:** Published — first statistically significant US field study  

---

## Abstract

INTRODUCTION: In 2018, AAP updated CSS guidelines to recommend rear-facing as long as possible; observational evidence remains limited.

METHODS: All MVCs involving children aged 0-4 reported to Kansas DOT 2011-2020. Rear-facing CSS compared with forward-facing device. Logistic regression for confounders.

RESULTS:
- Unadjusted: OR 0.860 (95% CI 0.805-0.919) — 14% reduction in any injury
- Adjusted: OR 0.909 (95% CI 0.840-0.983) — 9% reduction in any injury
- Results driven by back outboard seating positions
- Incapacitating/fatal injuries: negatively associated but estimates imprecise

CONCLUSIONS: Children aged 0-4 less likely to be injured in MVC when restrained rear-facing. Particularly relevant given many state laws do not require rear-facing at any age.

CONFLICT OF INTEREST: None declared.

---

## Methodology Notes & Limitations

- State-level administrative crash data (NOT nationally representative)
- Largest US field study to achieve statistical significance
- Kansas is NOT demographically representative of the US (less urban, less diverse)
- Adjusted covariates unstated in abstract — important gap
- "Driven by back outboard positions" — suggests seating position is the dominant variable
- Incapacitating/fatal estimates are imprecise (underpowered for severe outcomes)
- No demographic breakdown (race/ethnicity, income, family size) reported
