# Rear-Facing versus Forward-Facing Child Restraints: An Updated Assessment

**Authors:** McMurry TL, Arbogast KB, Sherwood CP, Vaca F, Bull M, Crandall JR, Kent RW  
**Published:** Inj Prev. 2018 Feb;24(1):55-59  
**DOI:** 10.1136/injuryprev-2017-042512  
**PMID:** 29175832  
**Source URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29175832/  
**Data Source:** NASS-CDS 1988-2015  
**Status:** Published (companion to Henary 2007 retraction)  

---

## Abstract

OBJECTIVES: Re-evaluated field evidence for RFCRS using NASS-CDS due to concerns raised by an independent researcher (JP Research).

METHODS: Children aged 0-1 year (0-23 months) in RFCRS or FFCRS selected from NASS-CDS 1988-2003 (replication) and 1988-2015 (updated). Survey-weighted chi-squared tests, injury rate comparison.

RESULTS: Years 1988-2015 contained 1,107 children aged 0-1 meeting inclusion criteria. Of these, 47 sustained ISS ≥ 9. Both age groups showed lower injury rates in RFCRS than FFCRS, but sample size was too small for reasonable statistical power or meaningful regression with covariates.

CONCLUSIONS: "Non-US field data and laboratory tests support the recommendation that children be kept in RFCRS for as long as possible, but the US NASS-CDS field data are too limited to serve as a strong statistical basis for these recommendations."

CONFLICT OF INTEREST: None declared.

## Methodology Notes & Limitations

- Same research team as Henary 2007 (retracted)
- Published in same issue (24:1) as retraction notice (e2)
- Key finding: n=47 serious injuries total across 27 years of data — severely underpowered
- No demographic covariates could be controlled (underpowered)
- Swedish and lab data cited as primary support for recommendation
